## In plain English

If you have more than one debt, you can get out faster by **focusing**. Pay the minimum on everything, then put every extra dollar toward **one** debt. When that one's gone, add its payment to the next one. Your payments "roll" forward and get bigger as you go.

There are two popular ways to choose which debt goes first:

- **Avalanche:** highest interest rate first. This saves the most money.
- **Snowball:** smallest balance first. You knock out whole debts sooner, which helps many people stay motivated.

Either one beats paying only the minimums.

## When to use it

- Finding your debt-free date
- Deciding between Avalanche and Snowball
- Seeing what difference an extra $50 or $100 a month makes

## Example

A **$1,000** credit card at **24%** APR adds about **$20** of interest in the first month.

- Pay **$15** a month and the balance actually _grows_ — it will **never** be paid off.
- Pay **$50** a month and it's gone in **26 months**, with about **$290** of interest.

## Watch out for

**Spending the money you free up.** The plan works because each paid-off debt's payment moves to the next one. If that money gets spent instead, it takes much longer. Also try not to add new charges to cards you're paying off.

<details>
<summary>The math (optional)</summary>

Each month, for each debt: **interest = balance × APR ÷ 12**. Then the minimums are paid, and the rest of your budget goes to the debt you're focusing on.

If your total payment isn't more than the monthly interest, the balance can't go down — so the calculator warns you instead of showing a payoff date.

</details>
