## What it is

The **50/30/20 rule** is a simple starting budget: of your take-home pay, about 50% goes to **needs**, 30% to **wants** and 20% to **savings** (including paying down debt beyond the minimums). You can adjust the split — it just has to add up to 100%.

An **emergency fund** is cash set aside for surprises like a job loss or a big repair, so they don't end up on a credit card.

## The formula

- Each bucket = take-home pay × its percentage
- **Emergency fund target = essential monthly expenses × months of coverage** (usually 3–6, more if your income is irregular)
- **Months to goal = (target − already saved) ÷ monthly saving**, rounded up

## Worked example

Take-home pay of $5,000 a month → $2,500 needs, $1,500 wants, $1,000 savings.

With $3,000 of essential expenses, a 6-month fund is **$18,000**. Having $2,000 saved and putting $1,000 a month toward it, you'll be fully funded in 16 months.

## A common mistake

**Budgeting from gross pay.** Use what actually lands in your account after taxes and deductions — otherwise every bucket looks bigger than the money you really have.
