## In plain English

The **50/30/20 rule** is an easy way to start a budget. Split your take-home pay into:

- **50% needs** — rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments
- **30% wants** — eating out, streaming, shopping, travel
- **20% savings** — your emergency fund, retirement, and paying off debt faster

It's a starting point, not a strict rule — change the split to fit your life, as long as it adds up to 100%.

An **emergency fund** is money set aside only for surprises — a job loss, a medical bill, a car repair — so they don't end up on a credit card. Most people aim for **3 to 6 months** of essential costs.

## When to use it

- Making your first budget
- Checking whether your spending is balanced
- Setting an emergency fund goal and timeline

## Example

You take home **$5,000** a month. That's **$2,500** for needs, **$1,500** for wants and **$1,000** for savings.

Your essential bills are **$3,000** a month, so a 6-month emergency fund is **$18,000**. With $2,000 already saved and $1,000 a month going toward it, you'd be fully covered in **16 months**.

## Watch out for

**Budgeting from your salary instead of your paycheck.** Use what actually lands in your bank account after taxes and deductions — otherwise every number looks bigger than the money you really have.

<details>
<summary>The math (optional)</summary>

- Each part = take-home pay × its percentage
- **Emergency fund goal = essential monthly costs × months to cover**
- **Months to goal = (goal − already saved) ÷ monthly saving**, rounded up
