## In plain English

With simple interest, you pay (or earn) a percentage of the **original amount only** — the interest never earns interest of its own. It's the simplest way to see how a percentage rate turns into real dollars.

## When to use it

- Understanding what "5% a year" really means in dollars
- Some car loans, short-term loans and bonds
- Quick checks before learning about compound interest

## Example

Borrow **$1,000** at **5%** a year for **3 years**.

Each year's interest is 5% of $1,000 = $50. Over 3 years that's **$150.00** of interest, so you'd pay back **$1,150.00** in total.

## Watch out for

**The 360-day year.** Some lenders treat a year as 360 days instead of 365, which quietly makes the interest about 1.4% higher. On a $100,000 loan at 8% for 90 days, that's $2,000.00 instead of $1,972.60.

<details>
<summary>The math (optional)</summary>

**Interest = Principal × Rate × Time** (often written **I = P × r × t**)

- **P** — the original amount
- **r** — the yearly rate as a decimal (5% → 0.05)
- **t** — time in years (months ÷ 12, or days ÷ 365 or 360)

</details>
