## What it is

Simple interest is charged (or earned) only on the original principal. Unlike compound interest, the interest never earns interest of its own, so the amount grows in a straight line.

You'll see simple interest on many auto loans, some personal and short-term business loans, bonds' coupon payments, and certificates that pay interest out rather than reinvesting it.

## The formula

**I = P × r × t**, and the total is **P + I**.

- **P** — principal
- **r** — annual rate as a decimal
- **t** — time in years. Months are divided by 12; days by 365 (actual) or 360 (the "banker's year").

## Worked example

$1,000 at 5% for 3 years: `1,000 × 0.05 × 3 = $150.00` of interest, for $1,150.00 in total.

## A common mistake

**Ignoring the day-count basis.** A lender who divides by 360 instead of 365 charges about 1.4% more interest for the same quoted rate. On a $100,000 loan at 8% for 90 days, that's $2,000.00 versus $1,972.60.
