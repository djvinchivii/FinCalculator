## In plain English

Investments go up and down — up 20% one year, down 10% the next. That makes it hard to say how well they really did.

**CAGR** (compound annual growth rate) answers that with one number: the **steady yearly growth rate** that would have taken you from your starting value to your ending value. It's the fairest way to compare two investments over time.

## When to use it

- Finding out how well an investment really did
- Comparing two investments held for different lengths of time
- Seeing how bumpy the ride was, using your yearly values

## Example

**$1,000** that grows to **$2,000** in **5 years** grew at a CAGR of **14.87%** a year. In other words, earning exactly 14.87% every single year would have gotten you to the same place.

## Watch out for

**Averaging the yearly returns.** Say an investment goes **up 50%** one year and **down 50%** the next. The average is 0%, which sounds like you broke even — but $100 → $150 → $75 means you actually **lost 25%**. CAGR tells the truth: about −13.4% a year.

<details>
<summary>The math (optional)</summary>

**CAGR = (Ending value ÷ Starting value)^(1 ÷ years) − 1**

With yearly values, the calculator also shows each year's return, the average, and the volatility (how much the yearly returns jump around).
