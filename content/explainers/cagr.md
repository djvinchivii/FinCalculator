## What it is

The **compound annual growth rate (CAGR)** is the steady yearly rate that would take an investment from its starting value to its ending value. Real investments zig-zag; CAGR smooths the path into one honest number you can compare across investments and time periods.

## The formula

**CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1 ÷ years) − 1**

Enter yearly values to also see the bumpy year-by-year returns, their volatility (standard deviation), and the smooth CAGR line on the same chart.

## Worked example

$1,000 that grows to $2,000 in 5 years: `(2,000 ÷ 1,000)^(1/5) − 1 = 14.87%` a year.

## A common mistake

**Averaging yearly returns.** If an investment gains 50% one year and loses 50% the next, the average return is 0% — but you actually lost 25% (`1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75`). The CAGR, −13.4% a year, tells the truth.
