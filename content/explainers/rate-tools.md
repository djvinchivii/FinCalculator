## In plain English

Rates can be shown in two ways, which makes them confusing to compare:

- **APR** is the basic yearly rate. Loans and credit cards show APR.
- **APY** also counts the interest you earn (or pay) on interest during the year, so it's a little higher. Savings accounts show APY.

The **Rule of 72** is a handy shortcut: divide 72 by a yearly rate to see roughly how many years it takes to double your money.

## When to use it

- Comparing a savings account (APY) with a loan (APR) fairly
- Understanding why a credit card's 24% APR costs you more than 24% a year
- Quickly estimating how long it takes money to double

## Example

A **5% APR** with interest added monthly works out to a **5.12% APY** (5.1162% exactly).

At **8%** a year, the Rule of 72 says your money doubles in 72 ÷ 8 = **9 years**. The exact answer is **9.006 years** — almost the same. The shortcut is less accurate at very low or very high rates: at 1% it says 72 years (really about 70), and at 30% it says 2.4 years (really about 2.6).

## Watch out for

**Comparing an APR to an APY.** Banks show savings accounts by APY (the bigger number) and loans by APR (the smaller number). Convert them to the same type before comparing.

<details>
<summary>The math (optional)</summary>

- **APY = (1 + APR ÷ n)^n − 1**, where _n_ is how many times a year interest is added
- **APR = n × ((1 + APY)^(1/n) − 1)**
- Continuous compounding: **APY = e^APR − 1**
- Exact doubling time: **ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + rate)**; Rule of 72: **72 ÷ rate in percent**

</details>
