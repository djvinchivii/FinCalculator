## What it is

**APR** (annual percentage rate) is the stated yearly rate. **APY** (annual percentage yield) is what you actually earn or pay in a year once compounding is included. The more often interest compounds, the bigger the gap.

The **Rule of 72** is a mental shortcut for how long money takes to double: divide 72 by the annual percentage rate.

## The formula

- **APY = (1 + APR/n)^n − 1**
- **APR = n × ((1 + APY)^(1/n) − 1)**
- Continuous compounding: **APY = e^APR − 1**
- Exact doubling time: **ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + r)**; Rule of 72: **72 ÷ (r × 100)**

## Worked example

A 5% APR compounded monthly: `(1 + 0.05/12)^12 − 1 = 5.1162%` APY.

At 8% a year, the Rule of 72 says money doubles in **9.00 years**; the exact answer is **9.006 years** — nearly identical. At 1% the rule says 72 years but the truth is 69.7; at 30% it says 2.4 years but the truth is 2.64.

## A common mistake

**Comparing an APR to an APY.** Banks advertise savings accounts by APY (the bigger number) and loans by APR (the smaller one). To compare fairly, convert both to the same measure.
