## What it is

Compound interest is interest earned on your interest. Each time interest is added to your balance, the next round of interest is calculated on the bigger balance — so growth speeds up the longer you leave the money alone.

## The formula

For a single deposit: **A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n × t)**

- **P** — what you start with
- **r** — the annual rate as a decimal (7% → 0.07)
- **n** — how many times a year interest compounds
- **t** — years

With continuous compounding it becomes **A = P × e^(r × t)**.

When you also add money regularly, this calculator simulates every contribution period one by one, using the effective rate per period `(1 + r/n)^(n/p) − 1` (where _p_ is how often you contribute). That's what lets it handle contributions that grow each year or happen on a different schedule than compounding.

## Worked example

$10,000 at 5% compounded monthly for 10 years:

`10,000 × (1 + 0.05/12)^(12 × 10) = $16,470.09`

You earned $6,470.09 without adding another cent. Add $500 a month at 7% for 30 years, starting from $0, and you end with about **$609,985** — even though you only put in $180,000.

## A common mistake

**Waiting to start.** Time is the most powerful input. Investing $500 a month from age 25 to 65 at 7% grows to about $1.3 million; starting at 35 gives about $610,000 — less than half, for only 25% fewer contributions.
