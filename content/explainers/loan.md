## In plain English

Most loans — car loans, personal loans, student loans — are paid back with the **same payment every month**. Each payment covers that month's interest first, and whatever is left pays down what you owe.

At the start, most of your payment goes to interest. As you owe less, more and more of each payment goes toward the loan itself. The schedule table shows this payment by payment.

## When to use it

- Checking whether a monthly payment fits your budget before you borrow
- Comparing a shorter loan with a longer one
- Seeing how much you'd save by paying a little extra

## Example

Borrow **$200,000** at **6%** for **30 years**. Your payment is **$1,199.10** a month.

Over 30 years you'd pay about **$231,677** in interest — more than you borrowed. Pay just **$200 extra** each month and you'd finish **9 years early** and save about **$79,800**.

## Watch out for

**Only looking at the monthly payment.** A longer loan has a smaller payment, but you pay interest for more years — often far more in total. Always check "Total interest" too. And before paying extra, make sure your lender doesn't charge a fee for paying early.

<details>
<summary>The math (optional)</summary>

**Payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**

- **P** — the amount borrowed
- **r** — the rate per payment (yearly rate ÷ payments per year; 6% monthly → 0.005)
- **n** — the total number of payments (30 years × 12 = 360)

With a 0% rate, the payment is just the amount ÷ the number of payments. Each row is rounded to the cent, and the last payment is adjusted by a few cents so you end at exactly $0.00.
