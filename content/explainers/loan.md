## What it is

An amortizing loan is paid off with equal payments. Early payments are mostly interest; as the balance shrinks, more of each payment goes to principal. The **amortization schedule** shows exactly how every payment is split.

## The formula

**M = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**

- **M** — the payment each period
- **P** — the amount borrowed
- **r** — the rate per payment (annual rate ÷ payments per year)
- **n** — the total number of payments

If the rate is 0%, the payment is simply **P ÷ n**.

Each period, interest = balance × r (rounded to the cent), and the rest of the payment reduces the balance. The final payment is adjusted by a few cents so the balance lands at exactly $0.00.

## Worked example

$200,000 at 6% for 30 years, paid monthly: r = 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005 and n = 360.

`200,000 × 0.005 / (1 − 1.005^−360) = $1,199.10` a month.

Over 30 years you pay about **$231,677** in interest — more than the loan itself. Adding just $200 a month to principal pays it off 9 years early and saves about $79,800.

## A common mistake

**Only comparing monthly payments.** A longer term lowers the payment but can double the total interest. Always look at the total cost, and check whether your lender allows extra principal payments without penalty.
