## In plain English

Inflation means prices go up over time. A coffee that costs $3 today might cost $4 in ten years. So the same dollar buys a little less each year.

This matters for two reasons:

- Cash sitting under the mattress (or in a 0% account) slowly **loses buying power**.
- An investment only makes you richer if it grows **faster than prices rise**.

## When to use it

- Seeing what something will cost in the future
- Understanding why keeping lots of cash long-term costs you
- Finding your real return — how much richer your investments actually make you

## Example

Something that costs **$100** today, with prices rising **3%** a year, will cost about **$134.39** in **10 years**.

If your investments grow **7%** a year and prices rise **3%**, your real growth is about **3.88%** a year — not quite the 4% you'd get by subtracting.

## Watch out for

**Thinking a savings account always "keeps you even."** If your account pays 1% and prices rise 3%, you're losing about 2% of buying power every year — even though the number in your account goes up.

<details>
<summary>The math (optional)</summary>

- **Future cost = today's price × (1 + inflation)^years**
- **Buying power = amount ÷ (1 + inflation)^years**
- **Real return = (1 + investment return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1** — called the Fisher equation. Simply subtracting the two rates is a rough shortcut that always overstates it a little.

</details>
