## What it is

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. It means a dollar buys less every year — so money sitting in cash quietly loses value, and investment returns must be judged _after_ inflation.

## The formula

- **Future cost = today's price × (1 + inflation)^years**
- **Purchasing power = amount ÷ (1 + inflation)^years**
- **Real return = (1 + nominal return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1** (the Fisher equation)

## Worked example

Something costing $100 today, at 3% inflation, costs `100 × 1.03^10 = $134.39` in 10 years.

If your investments earn 7% while inflation is 3%, your real return is `1.07 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = 3.8835%` — not the 4% you'd get by simply subtracting.

## A common mistake

**Subtracting instead of dividing.** "7% − 3% = 4%" is a fine rough guess at low rates, but it always overstates your real return, and the error grows with higher rates and longer horizons.
